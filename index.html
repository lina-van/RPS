A better way to understand recruiting capacity
Managing capacity by the effort work requires, not by counting it.

What you're looking at

Two recruiters' workloads. Today we compare them by counting reqs. RPS compares them by the hours of work those reqs still need, based on how complex each req is and how long it has been open.

Drag the sliders, then watch the red and navy bars below

  1. Number of reqs: add or remove reqs from a workload.
  2. Complexity: slide from Standard to Moderate to Complex. Harder roles take more hours.
  3. How long open: slide from Wk 1 to 10+. New reqs need the most work; older reqs need less.

Complexity

Standard×1.0Moderate×1.25Complex×1.5

An optional rubric creates a consistent language for effort.

Standard ×1.0: Straightforward to fill. Typical effort, clear requirements and a strong candidate pool. About 38 hours of work.
Moderate ×1.25: Some extra effort from sourcing, role requirements or stakeholder alignment. About 47 hours.
Complex ×1.5: High effort. Limited talent, specialized requirements or complexity across the search. About 57 hours.
The multiplier scales a standard req's hours. Complexity is about effort, not job level.

Capacity is personal, not a flat assumption.

Each workday counts as 8 hours. Time off and project work come out first, so capacity reflects the hours someone actually has for recruiting.

Try it: what to do

This is one sample recruiter's workload. The gauge on the right shows how full their capacity is. Try these and watch the gauge move:

  1. Change a req's complexity from Standard to Complex. Its hours go up.
  2. Change a req's age from Wk 1 to 10+. Its hours drop, because older reqs need less work.
  3. Click “+ Add req” a few times. See how quickly new reqs push the gauge toward yellow and red.
  4. Add PTO or project hours below. Same reqs, less time, so capacity fills up.

Click Reset anytime to start over.
